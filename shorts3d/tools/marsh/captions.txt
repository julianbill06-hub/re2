One kid. One marshmallow. And one single rule.
If you wait, you get two.
Then the researcher leaves the room.
This is the famous marshmallow test. Stanford, late 1960s.
Psychologist Walter Mischel wanted to know: how long can kids hold out?
The kids were about four or five years old. The wait: up to fifteen minutes.
Some ate the marshmallow right away.
Others covered their eyes, sang songs or simply turned away.
And then came the surprise.
Years later, the researchers checked how the kids turned out.
Those who waited longer did better on school tests, on average.
Patience as a superpower?
Not so fast.
A bigger study in 2018 found: the link is much weaker.
Things like family background mattered much more.
And another experiment showed: when kids don't trust the adult, they eat right away.
