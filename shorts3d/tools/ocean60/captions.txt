Thousands of soldiers. Catapults. An emperor ready to attack.
The enemy?
The ocean.
In 40 AD, Caligula lined up his army on the coast of Gaul.
Across the water lay Britain. Everyone waited for the order to sail.
They stood there in full armor. Ready for the big invasion.
Then the trumpets sounded.
And Caligula shouted: collect seashells!
The soldiers looked at each other. Did they hear that right?
But an order is an order. They filled their helmets with shells.
Ancient writers say Caligula called them: spoils of the ocean.
He even built a tall tower as a monument to his victory.
And every soldier got a reward.
Then he marched back to Rome like a winner.
But was he really mad?
Some historians think the soldiers had refused to cross.
Then the seashells were a punishment.
