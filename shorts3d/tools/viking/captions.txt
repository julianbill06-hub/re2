Everyone knows this Viking. But he's made up.
The horns?
No Viking ever wore them.
Over 1,000 years ago, the Vikings set sail. To England, France, even America.
They fought with axes, spears and round wooden shields.
But horns on a helmet? In battle they'd just get in the way.
The proof? In 1943, a farmer in Norway digs into a mound.
Inside: a real Viking helmet. Round, iron, with eye guards.
And no horns.
So where does the myth come from?
From Germany. 1876, in Bayreuth.
Richard Wagner stages his giant opera: The Ring of the Nibelung.
Costume designer Carl Emil Doepler gives some characters horned helmets.
The audience loves it. Painters, books and later movies copy it.
Real horned helmets? They did exist.
But in the Bronze Age. Over 1,500 years before the Vikings.
So the world's most famous Viking comes from an opera.
